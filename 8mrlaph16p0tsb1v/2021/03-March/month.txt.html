# March 7

Wasted most of the day depressed and completely sure that I would amount—and have, to this point, amounted—to nothing.

That kind of day.

## Choosing one's tools

Rebecca took the children to HV for fish supper. I stayed home and watched DOOM emacs tutorials, thinking that this is the kind of person I need to be. I need to be someone that uses tools first developed for a time that cannot imagine the technology of today. 

I teach the command line. I find value in the environment, but I would be lying if I said I did not like this. I write in plain text. That holds true at this moment as I use iA Writer for Windows. It looks fucking great.

The typing experience is phenomenal. The entire screen is mine to fill.

I have written more in these 5 minutes than I did in all the time spend wishing I was an emacs wizard. (*sigh* these are true things!)

## George Saunders

Currently, I am taking a Russian short story course from George Saunders. HOW FUCKING WONDERFUL DOES THAT SOUND?!

Well, it's true. The communication is one way, but I am all in. *A Swim in a Pond in the Rain* is his latest book. It is a condensed university course in a book. (What I wouldn't give for such a course from my former teachers, Fred White and Stuart Durant).

I should email them sometime just to let them know that I think about them. That's not what I'll say, but it's what will be felt. By them. From what I say.

# 20210311

The buttermilk chicken, a recipe/method I picked up from Samin Nosrat's *Salt Fat Acid Heat*, is in the fridge. One of the handful of kitchen skills that I have perfected during the pandemic.

1. Whole Chicken, wing tips cut off, salted. Rest for 20–time-it-takes-to-do-bedtime minutes.
2. Put chicken in ziploc bag with 500mL of buttermilk + 2 tbsp coarse kosher salt.
3. Place in fridge for 24 hours.
4. Take out of fridge 30–60 minutes before cooking.
5. Pre-heat oven to 450F
6. Place Chicken in cast-iron skillet, wiping off a good deal of buttermilk as you take it out of the bag.
7. Place in oven for 50-55 minutes; rotate once or twice if you care about that sort of thing.

Makes for some great gravy. Simple corn-starch slurry works. A roux will also do.
