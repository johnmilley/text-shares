### Today, Better

Woke up with aches and pains. Plowed through making the kids lunch, coffee, and smoothies (poor reviews from M on those this morning, need to ditch the mixed berries for strawberries).

Lawrence helped me this morning. The other day (...Sunday?) we were playing on the playroom floor. I was the crab, Lawrence the spider. Each was represented by a small plastic toy of the same. The spider was a part of a costume belonging to a halloween themed _Lego_ figure. I am not sure where the little crab came from, but it was rubbery, lifelike, and of almost identical size to the spider. We acted the voices and actions of these two creatures to his great amusement. I was in a state of exhaustion, but still really enjoying the moment.

I found the spider outside on the loose gravel of our walkway this morning. He had mentioned earlier in the week that he had took it outside and lost it. Stumbling upon it this morning was the first crack in my shell. I left it on the kitchen table and hurried off with Theodore and Marianne to meet the bus at the end of the street. When I got home he was playing with the spider on his stool at the counter.

We played... well mostly, he played, happily though and not feeling ignored... through my breakfast and my 15 minutes of _restorative_ yoga (quad focused). I got in the shower and he followed, still playing with the spider. Out of the shower, I noticed the crab on my nightstand (of course he's there! ???...). Lawrence was joyful at the discovery, the second of this new day.

He brought spider tucked into his shirt pocket to daycare. A little bending of the rules.

### Things, they just don't come.

In a mood, I am.

Ok. So, my shell wasn't as hard as I thought. Today, it has many cracks and missing pieces. I know how quickly it can be rebuilt. I have done it many times before. Some part of me, I want to say a bad part of me, but I also know that that is not fully the truth as the shell does provide some form of protection. Or does it? Is it more like a shitty chrysalis that could potentially turn me on a worse path? I feel like that's a possibility. I know very little, it seems, but feel quite a lot.

Anyways, the point of this entry was to remind myself that creativity happens in the doing. Waiting for something to happen is a bad way to live and I often live that way. My creativity has been poor to non-exisitant throughout COVID. It was poor before COVID as well.

- I rarely write in my journals (physical, digital)
- I am not drawing in my field notes / little books
- I own unopened paints and paintbrushes
- I read very little
- I have little focus
- No programming projects 'on the go' (Need to work on Nan's Recipe site)

There have been some periodic creativity spurts over the last 1.5–2 years, but it's mostly low.

I need to develop systems that encourage creativity and reflection. I have this journal set up currently to do just that and it is getting me to write more habitually. Today's output is uncommon, but very welcome. I'm hopeful that this machine can keep it going.

iPad + Magic Keyboard is a great tool for writing and other creative work. It's an ecosystem that fosters creativity. I work on a desktop computer for a living. That's not the type of ecosystem that I want to use outside of work. This is it.

Ok. I'm going off the rails with this. My point: if you want to read, you need to read. If you want to write, sit down and write. If you want to exercise, go and exercise right now. You cannot wait for motivation, that only comes after you have begun something. Motivation is a feeling of furthering, not of beginning.
