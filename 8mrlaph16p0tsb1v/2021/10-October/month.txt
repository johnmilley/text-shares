The send off today was beautiful.
Rebecca had got me to rush out before the ceremony started to get my water bottle from the van. I am glad I forgot it. As I rushed out the front door the bells began tolling. I jogged briskly to the car in my seldom worn funeral shoes. I grabbed the water from the trunk of the van. I had remembered seeing it that morning, and had taken note of its location, knowing that we would have need of it at some time. 
The need was now.
As I was saying, I grabbed the water and began another brisk jog back to the church, greeting a local on the way to his vehicle for reasons that will likely remain unknown.
The bells were commanding. 
They rang for Esther.
They rang for no one else.
They filled my heart with purpose. Water for the eulogy giver.
I carried out my task proudly.
Those bells. Those bells. 
Ringing to the whims of the Ramea wind.
Constant for a time, softer all of a sudden, louder all of a sudden, then constant again for a few beats. The doors of the church closed behind me and the inside of the church filled me entirely.

The song book cover had an interior picture of the church we were sitting in. It was an empty church in the picture. All of the kids took note of that empty photo that showed the pew they now stood in for the first time.
