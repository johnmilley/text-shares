Maritime Vacation: Day 9

(Writing this a 4 days after based on quick jot notes made on the evening of Day 9)

Jot Notes

Saltwater kisses Sea shells M Cracked sea shell art Live Crab

Kings Pine

Sally's, Catch, Soccer, bike ride with M, fire, ghost pepper chips

From memory

The day started wonderfully. A bike ride to the beach (family via van). Dad, Preston, and Lucy joined us. The rest (not including mom — sick) went to Charlottetown for the morning.

The weather has been perfect the entire time.

Brackley Beach was busy by 10, but not overly so. Populated with like-minded vacationers. I combed the beach for shells and found a few. Each one that I found fit into a one of the other found shells. Ended up with 4 that stowed away nicely into the largest shell that required the most daring to retrieve (hardly any daring at all).

The water was healing. Cold with warm currents. Enveloping vigour and rejuvenation. I saw a crab scuttle away along the bottom, its image mirrored and wavy but clear from above the water. I walked away and called out to T who begged that I go back and catch it (to eat!). I was too far away to go back. Seconds later, T found one and called me over. R tried to pick it up and it pinched her. It pinched me too. I managed to get it on another try and held it up for all the kids to see. It was missing its right arm (I would have liked to see how that happened!)

R joined me in the water. I brought her down into the water and we shared a salt water kiss.

M created beautiful ephemeral art at the water's edge. Then we left.

If only every day could start that way. The bittersweetness of vacation highlights... of any highlight.

I texted Ben Smith the night before, letting him know that I played two of his courses — Rotary Park in Sydney and CDT in Moncton (Riverview). I let him know that I loved playing them both and that tomorrow (today) I would be playing Rose Valley. He suggested King's Pine instead and boy am I glad I took that advice. What a marvel. Beautiful rectangular wooded lot turned into what will be a premier disc golf destination in the not-too-distant future. The trees are tall and covering. The fairways are fair and interesting. The green growth and the elevation down to the elevated basket of 13 filled me with disc golf glee (it helps that I birdied it in spectacular fashion. Straight tee shot with Origin. Flex anhyzer PD to the basket.)

There was a pristine premium mid-range (Westside Warship) lying on the top of basket 10. It had a name on it. I took it. I also took a factory second Mako 3 from the clubhouse. That one had a hard to read name and number. I cannot justify why I did this. It was not a nice thing to do. Back in CB I had lost my (only ace) Kona Panis Mako3 and my Latitude Trust within a day of each other. Losing those discs bummed me out so much that I lost my enjoyment of the sport for a short time. The Mako3 especially was a special disc, not only for the ace. So yeah. No justification, just butthurt over not taking care of my own discs. Now I have passed on that hurt to others... but man do they both fly well! I love both of these discs. Used them both in my 48 round yesterday (2022-08-08). I don't regret taking them. That's an unfortunate part of who I am. It begun as a new teenager—the mischievous hobbit of stealing Calvin and Hobbes books (thanks, Bradford!). Sorry to everyone who has come up against that part of me.

King's Pine. Can't wait to play it again!
