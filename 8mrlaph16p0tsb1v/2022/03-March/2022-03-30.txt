# Keep on running

Some head thoughts:

I want to keep drinking on the weekends. 

That's just an objectively true statement. That's what I want. I'm still not convinced that I should. I don't want to live a life of not drinking... because I enjoy it. I enjoy the taste and I enjoy the feeling of calmness. The problems start when it gets outside of the weekend and becomes a part of daily life. That's the part I need to prevent.

Thing I know (and want) to do regularly:
- read books
- meditate
- yoga
- drink more tea
- code
- watch movies
- play video games

Since putting myself in the mindset of "I cannot drink anymore", my mind wants to do none of the above. It just wants to rage at the thought of depriving myself of that warm feeling of obliteration. I jest. It takes the edge off. Most times. When it doesn't, that's probably a good sign that I should not be doing it.

So many of the stories I read on the topic describe behaviors that I have rarely engaged in. Deceitful drinking. Yes, it did get to the point recently where I was downing half flasks secretly at my computer desk during the weekdays. That is objectively not a good thing. It is not what I want to do. I feel confident that I can stop that behavior. I have stopped that behavior. Also, those lapses do not last for long period of time—weeks, a month at most.

Drinking to blackout. I may have done this once or twice as a young 20-something—with predictably bad results. Never in my adult life have I blacked out. The worst I have done, and again we are talking about no more than a handful of times, is gotten a little too hungover in the morning—to the point where I was low functioning and well... a little sick. Those days sucked and I don't wish to repeat them. However, to my benefit, those times often happened during stressful events (deaths, weeks of sleeplessness, job loss). I am not someone who drinks to excess regularly. That's never been my problem.

I drink alone, regularly. I like the way it relaxes me and allows me to take in a movie. Combined with a little THC, it puts me in an open and warm receptive state. I have had blissful moments like this. 

Chasing those moments can be a problem. 

Drinking to ignore stresses is another problem.

That's why I need regular practices in my life.

1. **Morning Papers**: writing 3 pages of long-hand each morning before starting the day. Stream of consciousness. I began this routine today and I enjoyed it. I think this could be something worth sticking with. **15-20 minutes**
2. **Regular Meditation**: Trying Sam Harris' Wake Up app. Seems to jive with my initial introduction to mediation through Shambhala. **10-20 minutes**
3. **Yoga**: Regular yoga makes me feel great. It's a necessity. **15-25 minutes**
4. **Running**: I have been running now for about 10 years. It's good exercise that I know how to do. Keeping it to the gym treadmill for the winter/spring days, but once the snow melts I'll be back on the trails. **40 minutes**

Right now my thinking is that if I can manage to regularly engage in the above activities, I can enjoy a few drinks on the weekend. Why the fuck not.

1–3 I should do every day. 4, at least 5 days a week. Upwards of 1h 45m each day. My days are going to have to begin earlier for that to happen.
