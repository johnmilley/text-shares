# What We Leave Behind
October 19th

I was reading through the Western Star this morning and when I came to the “In Memoriam” section my heart sank and my brain raced.  Pictured were two young men who died much too early. Both men were fathers.  I'm writing this while my son, Theodore, sits on his Winnie the Pooh bouncey seat.  What will be left of me should I join the ranks of those two unfortunate men? 

Practically speaking, there are a few things that I can do:

- Write a will
- Buy a safe
- Try not to die

### Writing a Will

![Will writing](http://www.virginmedia.com/images/smith_signing_gal_hancock_3.jpg)

Writing a will can be done in one of three ways in NL. First, a holograph will is upheld in this province.  This is a document that is written entirely by hand by the testator (will-writer) and signed. No witnesses are required.  This could be useful in a pinch (if your pinned under a tractor for example), but having something a little more robust might be more useful.

Will kits are another economical option.  They generally come in at under $50.  The problems I'm reading about with these though is that they could potentially be thrown out or revoked by a court for a number of reasons.

Going to a lawyer seems like the safest bet, though, of course, the most costly.  For a simple estate like ours we'd be looking at $300-500. 

Right now I think the best thing to do would be to have a simple holograph on file somewhere and to research the matter further.

Another will to consider is the Advanced Health Care Directive (or living will, as it is more commonly known).  This document serves two important purposes:

1. What you want to happen if you are unable to make decisions for yourself
2. Who do you wish to give decision-making powers to.  

This will is easily done and only requires a frank discussion and the willingness of two people to witness the document.  A form version can be found [here](http://www.advancecareplanning.ca/media/52141/ahcd_booklet.pdf)

Once completed, make copies of the AHCD and give them to trusted relatives (parents).  Also, it might not be a bad idea to own a safe deposit box to hold this and other important documents. 

### Safes

![A well-reviewd safe available at staples.ca for ~$150 -- SentrySafe® X125 Security Safe](http://www.johnmilley.com/images/home_safe.jpg)

Again, like a will, one option would be to pay someone for this service; in this case, a bank. Renting a small safe deposit box from a bank costs $50-100 per year.  Given my current lack of gold and precious gems, I don't believe this is the best option for me.  

A quick google search displays a variety of affordable fire-proof safes.  They range from $20-500 and more.  Theiving is not something I'm too concerned about for the time being.  I live in a small town and the box would only contain the documents mentioned above (of which copies had already been made and distributed) and some items of mostly sentimental value.
